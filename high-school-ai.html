Grades 9–12

AI Innovators: Preparing Students to Understand, Evaluate, and Lead With AI

The AI Innovators pathway helps high school students move beyond basic tool use. Through relevant, case-based learning, students examine how artificial intelligence works, evaluate information and evidence, recognize bias, protect privacy, apply human judgment, and connect AI literacy to college, career, and civic readiness.

Student Framework

Five Competencies for Responsible AI Literacy

Understand

Understand what AI systems do, how they use data, and how they generate predictions, recommendations, or content.

Evaluate

Evaluate accuracy, source evidence, limitations, and whether AI-generated information can be trusted.

Analyze

Recognize bias, missing context, unequal impact, and potential harm.

Protect

Protect privacy, safeguard sensitive information, and use AI responsibly.

Apply

Apply human judgment, communicate decisions, and remain accountable for outcomes.

Curriculum Design

AI Innovators Curriculum Strands

The pathway can be delivered as a sequence, integrated into existing courses, or introduced through selected lessons and interdisciplinary projects.

AI Foundations

Core concepts, systems, data, predictions, and the basic building blocks of artificial intelligence.

Media Literacy

Evaluating AI-generated content, misinformation, source credibility, accuracy, and evidence.

Bias and Fairness

Examining data, representation, unequal outcomes, missing context, and equitable decision-making.

Digital Citizenship

Privacy, safety, disclosure, academic integrity, appropriate use, and responsible participation.

Generative AI

Creative problem-solving, responsible prompting, verification, revision, and thoughtful human-AI collaboration.

Career and CTE Connections

Real-world applications across business, technology, media, health science, engineering, manufacturing, transportation, construction, drones, and aviation.

AI Governance and Human Oversight

Transparency, accountability, appeal processes, responsible decision-making, and the role of human judgment.

Career Readiness

AI Literacy Across Career Pathways

AI literacy is not limited to computer science. Students need the ability to evaluate information, protect data, communicate clearly, and apply professional judgment across every career field.

Business and Entrepreneurship

Analyze market information, review AI-generated business recommendations, and communicate evidence-based decisions.

Computer Science

Examine data, models, testing, bias, system limitations, and responsible development.

Digital Media

Evaluate synthetic media, authorship, accuracy, disclosure, and audience impact.

Health Science

Explore privacy, sensitive data, decision support, and the need for professional oversight.

Engineering and Manufacturing

Use AI-supported analysis and design while validating outputs and safety requirements.

Transportation

Examine predictive systems, maintenance data, routing, safety, and human accountability.

Construction

Explore project planning, site documentation, risk identification, and responsible technology use.

Drone and Aviation Pathways

Connect AI literacy with mission planning, aerial data, mapping, safety, and human decision-making.

Flagship High School Lesson

Who Is Responsible When AI Makes a Decision?

In this grades 9–12 case-based lesson, students investigate a fictional AI-supported internship recommendation system. They examine the purpose of the system, the information it uses, who may be affected, what may be missing or unfair, and where human review is required.

Grades 9–12 Approximately 60 Minutes Case-Based Learning Ethical Reasoning Collaborative Analysis Responsible AI Assessment and Reflection
View the Full Lesson Details

Students Will

  • identify the purpose of an AI-supported decision system
  • evaluate the data and evidence used
  • identify missing information and possible unfairness
  • examine stakeholder perspectives
  • recommend safeguards and human oversight
  • explain who should remain accountable for the final decision

Student Tool

Five Questions for Responsible AI Decisions

A reusable framework students apply whenever they evaluate an AI-supported decision, tool, or recommendation.

Purpose

What decision or problem is the AI system supporting?

Data

What information is being used, and is it accurate and relevant?

Impact

Who could benefit, be excluded, or be harmed?

Fairness and Transparency

What may be missing, biased, unclear, or difficult to challenge?

Human Oversight

Who reviews the result, who has final authority, and how can a decision be appealed?
