Welcome to the Future Ready AI curriculum.

Our K–12 learning pathways help students understand AI, use it responsibly, and apply it with creativity and human judgment.

AI Discoverers supports elementary learners, AI Explorers supports middle school, and AI Innovators supports high school.

Each pathway builds AI literacy, digital citizenship, critical thinking, and responsible decision-making.

Learning Objectives

The Six Future Ready AI Curriculum Pillars

AI Foundations

Understanding what artificial intelligence is, how it works, where it appears, and what its limitations are.

Creativity and Innovation

Using AI thoughtfully to support inquiry, design, communication, creativity, and problem-solving.

Responsible and Ethical AI

Examining privacy, bias, fairness, transparency, accountability, and human judgment.

Prompting and Communication

Evaluating accuracy, recognizing manipulation, verifying information, and identifying missing perspectives.

Building responsible technology habits while exploring careers, leadership, and emerging opportunities.

Standards-informed design

Built on recognized frameworks

Future Ready AI lessons are designed to support skills reflected in technology, computer science, digital citizenship, media literacy, communication, and career-readiness frameworks. The curriculum is informed by the ISTE Standards for Students, the CSTA K–12 Computer Science Standards, the AI4K12 Five Big Ideas in AI, and digital citizenship and media-literacy competencies, and draws on responsible-AI guidance from UNESCO and the voluntary NIST AI Risk Management Framework. These frameworks inform the design; referencing them does not indicate endorsement, certification, or a completed formal alignment. Formal standards crosswalks may be developed for district or state implementation requirements.

  • Education technology standardsISTE Standards for Students
  • Model computer science standardsCSTA K–12 Computer Science Standards
  • AI literacy guidelinesAI4K12 Five Big Ideas in AI
  • CompetenciesDigital citizenship & media literacy
  • International guidance (non-binding)UNESCO
  • Voluntary federal framework — not lawNIST AI Risk Management Framework

Need a crosswalk for your district or state? Tell us your requirements or book a discovery call.

Responsible AI & student privacy

Guardrails built into the curriculum

Policy Status & Responsible AI

Artificial intelligence law, regulation, government guidance, technical standards, and industry practices continue to evolve.

Future Ready AI distinguishes between:

  • binding legal requirements,
  • official government guidance,
  • voluntary government frameworks,
  • school or district policy, and
  • emerging industry practices.

Voluntary frameworks and industry practices are not presented as law.

Educators and participating organizations remain responsible for following applicable federal, state, and local requirements and their own school or district policies for technology, privacy, accessibility, student accounts, and AI use.

Student Privacy & Technology Guardrails

Future Ready AI is designed around these principles:

  • No student AI account is required for core learning activities unless a school or district separately approves one.
  • Students are not required to enter personal or sensitive information into an AI system to demonstrate learning.
  • Lessons use fictional, teacher-approved, or appropriately de-identified information.
  • Students are not required to upload their face, voice, precise location, disability information, private schoolwork, or other sensitive information to participate.
  • Schools and educators use approved technology and follow local privacy and technology policies.
  • Tool-neutral and offline options remain available where the learning objective does not require a specific platform.
  • AI output is not treated as final authority for consequential decisions about students; human review, judgment, accountability, and correction remain central.

These design principles support responsible use; they do not by themselves establish legal compliance.

Inclusive by Design

Future Ready AI incorporates flexible access supports and multiple ways for students to engage with and demonstrate learning. These supports do not replace a student’s IEP, 504 Plan, or other documented accommodations. Educators should follow all individualized plans and local requirements.

Supported access options may include writing, drawing, dictation, pointing/selecting, sentence starters, reduced writing load, visual supports, teacher modeling, flexible response formats, scaffolded organizers, and extended processing time when appropriate.

Not every support is appropriate for every student; educators select supports based on each student’s documented needs.

Instructional Design

The Future Ready AI Learning Cycle

Launch

Introduce a question, scenario, challenge, or real-world problem.

Explore

Investigate examples, tools, ideas, evidence, and possible approaches.

Explain

Build understanding through discussion, modeling, and direct instruction.

Create

Apply learning through a product, solution, performance task, or prototype.

Reflect

Evaluate choices, outcomes, responsible use, and the role of human judgment.

Extend

Connect the learning to another subject, career, community, or emerging challenge.

Project-Based Learning

From lessons to portfolios, capstones, and transfer

Many units culminate in a multi-day or multi-week project — a student-designed AI ethics campaign, a class-built chatbot concept, or a research presentation — so students apply what they've learned to something real.

Elementary

An AI creativity showcase, a pattern investigation, or a responsible-technology poster.

Middle School

A media-verification campaign, a collaborative chatbot concept, or a digital-citizenship challenge.

High School

An ethics investigation, a career-connected challenge, a responsible drone mission, or a capstone innovation project.

Implementation Matters

The curriculum does not stop at the student workbook.

Future Ready AI is designed as an implementation system for schools—not a folder of disconnected lessons.

Protected teacher-only materials and answer keys are not published on the public website. Public pages show the architecture and selected shareable examples.

01

Curriculum & facilitator guidance

Scope and sequence, lesson architecture, teaching moves, safeguards, differentiation, and implementation notes.

02

Student evidence & assessment

Student workbooks, portfolio prompts, checks for understanding, rubrics, reflection, transfer tasks, and capstone resources.

03

Family & administrator support

Family-facing information, administrator/site pilot planning, technology expectations, and responsible-use communication.

04

Educator professional learning

A seven-session professional-learning architecture covering AI literacy, privacy and student safety, generative AI limitations, research and verification, assessment integrity, bias and fairness, and implementation planning.

05

Classroom & LMS implementation

Posting guidance, teacher-only controls, student-facing sequencing, accessibility checks, and pilot quality assurance.

Standards-informed design: Curriculum development references recognized AI literacy, digital citizenship, computer science, responsible AI, and local instructional requirements while remaining adaptable to school context.

Customization

Adapted to your school, not the other way around

Pacing, examples, and depth can be adjusted to match local standards, schedules, and student needs. Custom curriculum development is available for schools and districts with specific goals.
