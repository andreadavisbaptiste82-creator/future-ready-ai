Welcome to the Future Ready AI curriculum.

Our K–12 learning pathways help students understand AI, use it responsibly, and apply it with creativity and human judgment.

AI Discoverers supports elementary learners, AI Explorers supports middle school, and AI Innovators supports high school.

Each pathway builds AI literacy, digital citizenship, critical thinking, and responsible decision-making.

Let's explore the curriculum.

Grade Bands

Three pathways that deepen over time

The learning architecture becomes progressively more analytical, independent, and career-connected from elementary through high school.

Elementary core materials currently begin at Grades 3–5. K–2 educators receive adaptation guidance for teacher-led, developmentally appropriate use; a separate K–2 student workbook is not presented as a completed core pathway.

Learning Objectives

The Six Future Ready AI Curriculum Pillars

AI Foundations

Understanding what artificial intelligence is, how it works, where it appears, and what its limitations are.

Creativity and Innovation

Using AI thoughtfully to support inquiry, design, communication, creativity, and problem-solving.

Responsible and Ethical AI

Examining privacy, bias, fairness, transparency, accountability, and human judgment.

Prompting and Communication

Learning how questions, instructions, context, and revision influence AI outputs.

Critical Thinking and Media Literacy

Evaluating accuracy, recognizing manipulation, verifying information, and identifying missing perspectives.

Digital Citizenship and Future Readiness

Building responsible technology habits while exploring careers, leadership, and emerging opportunities.

Scope and Sequence

The Current Grade-Band Learning Sequences

These sequences reflect the current student-facing workbooks: six weeks for AI Discoverers, eight weeks for AI Explorers, and nine weeks for AI Innovators.

Grades 3–5 core • K–2 adaptation guidance • 45–60 minutes per week

Week 1 — AI Detectives: What Is AI?

Distinguish AI, fixed-rule tools, and cases where more evidence is needed; map input, pattern, output, and the person responsible.

Week 2 — Responsible AI Superpowers

Practice privacy, honesty, kindness, safe choices, and when to stop or check with a trusted adult.

Week 3 — Pattern Builders

Explore how labeled examples shape patterns and why incomplete examples can produce weak results.

Week 4 — Truth Trackers

Check confident-sounding claims with teacher-approved evidence and explain why an AI response is not its own proof.

Week 5 — Fairness Lab

Notice when a recommendation may work better for some people than others and suggest a fairer process.

Week 6 — Create for Good

Start with a real need, decide whether AI is appropriate, map human responsibility, and explain a helpful solution idea.

Grades 6–8 • Eight-week responsible AI portfolio

Week 1 — AI Systems Analysis: Inputs, Outputs & Human Oversight

Analyze how an AI-supported system transforms information and identify where people must remain responsible.

Week 2 — Data Quality & Representation Audit

Examine how training examples and missing representation can shape system performance.

Week 3 — Privacy, Consent & Data Minimization

Decide what information is actually necessary and design safer data practices.

Week 4 — Verification Lab: Claims, Sources & AI Reliability

Check AI-generated claims against evidence, sources, context, and uncertainty.

Week 5 — Algorithmic Fairness & Access

Analyze how seemingly neutral inputs can create unequal outcomes and propose safeguards.

Week 6 — Synthetic Media, Influence & Digital Citizenship

Evaluate potentially altered media before believing or sharing it and document verification decisions.

Week 7 — Human-Centered Problem Framing & AI Fit

Investigate an authentic need before deciding whether AI belongs in the solution.

Week 8 — Responsible AI Design Challenge

Refine a solution concept with minimum necessary data, human review, safeguards, limits, and accountability.

Grades 9–12 • Nine-week AI literacy and readiness pathway

Week 1 — AI Around Us: Orientation & Baseline

Map where AI influences daily life, establish baseline understanding, and identify where human choices shape systems.

Week 2 — AI Safety, Ethics & Responsible Use

Apply responsible-use expectations through scenario analysis, decision records, and a learning agreement.

Week 3 — How Generative AI Works—and Where It Fails

Explain generative AI in accessible language and analyze limitations, hallucinations, and unreliable outputs.

Week 4 — Prompting, Research & Verification

Create a verified brief, document evidence, and disclose meaningful AI assistance.

Week 5 — Bias, Fairness & AI-Supported Decisions

Examine stakeholder impact, missing context, fairness, and safeguards in an AI-supported decision.

Week 6 — Media Literacy & Synthetic Content

Verify synthetic or altered content and communicate appropriate disclosure.

Week 7 — Human-Centered Problem Discovery & AI Fit

Develop a problem statement, stakeholder map, and evidence-based test of whether AI is appropriate.

Week 8 — Responsible Solution Design & Career Connections

Create an AI role map, risk register, safeguards, and prototype while connecting work to career skills.

Week 9 — Capstone Pitch, Reflection & Post-Assessment

Defend the solution, evidence, limitations, safeguards, human authority, and next steps through a portfolio and pitch.

Instructional Design

The Future Ready AI Learning Cycle

Launch

Introduce a question, scenario, challenge, or real-world problem.

Explore

Investigate examples, tools, ideas, evidence, and possible approaches.

Explain

Build understanding through discussion, modeling, and direct instruction.

Create

Apply learning through a product, solution, performance task, or prototype.

Reflect

Evaluate choices, outcomes, responsible use, and the role of human judgment.

Extend

Connect the learning to another subject, career, community, or emerging challenge.

Additional Lesson Concepts

Additional pathway evidence

Spotting the Difference: AI or Human-Made?

Grade Band: Middle School Estimated Time: 45 minutes
Learning Objectives
Students distinguish common characteristics of AI-generated text and images from human-made work, and explain their reasoning.
Key Vocabulary
Artificial intelligence, generated content, bias, verification
Activity Overview
In small groups, students review paired examples of AI-generated and human-made content, discuss identifying clues, and present their reasoning to the class.
Responsible AI Connection
Reinforces the habit of evaluating AI-generated information rather than accepting it at face value.
Assessment Method
Group discussion rubric plus a short individual reflection.
Required Materials
Printed or projected content pairs (provided), discussion guide (provided).

Project-Based Learning

From lessons to portfolios, capstones, and transfer

Many units culminate in a multi-day or multi-week project — a student-designed AI ethics campaign, a class-built chatbot concept, or a research presentation — so students apply what they've learned to something real.

Middle School

High School

An ethics investigation, a career-connected challenge, a responsible drone mission, or a capstone innovation project.

Implementation Matters

The curriculum does not stop at the student workbook.

Future Ready AI is designed as an implementation system for schools—not a folder of disconnected lessons.

Protected teacher-only materials and answer keys are not published on the public website. Public pages show the architecture and selected shareable examples.

01

Curriculum & facilitator guidance

Scope and sequence, lesson architecture, teaching moves, safeguards, differentiation, and implementation notes.

02

Student evidence & assessment

Student workbooks, portfolio prompts, checks for understanding, rubrics, reflection, transfer tasks, and capstone resources.

03

Family & administrator support

Family-facing information, administrator/site pilot planning, technology expectations, and responsible-use communication.

04

Educator professional learning

A seven-session professional-learning architecture covering AI literacy, privacy and student safety, generative AI limitations, research and verification, assessment integrity, bias and fairness, and implementation planning.

05

Classroom & LMS implementation

Posting guidance, teacher-only controls, student-facing sequencing, accessibility checks, and pilot quality assurance.

Standards-informed design: Curriculum development references recognized AI literacy, digital citizenship, computer science, responsible AI, and local instructional requirements while remaining adaptable to school context.
