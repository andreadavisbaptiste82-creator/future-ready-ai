About this learning pathway

Welcome to AI Explorers.

Middle-school learners practice stronger prompting, source-checking, privacy protection, and recognizing bias.

Through collaboration and hands-on projects, students learn to question AI and use it thoughtfully.

Scope & sequence

What students learn, unit by unit

Grades 6–8 • Eight-week responsible AI portfolio

Week 1 — AI Systems Analysis: Inputs, Outputs & Human Oversight

Analyze how an AI-supported system transforms information and identify where people must remain responsible.

Week 2 — Data Quality & Representation Audit

Examine how training examples and missing representation can shape system performance.

Week 3 — Privacy, Consent & Data Minimization

Decide what information is actually necessary and design safer data practices.

Week 4 — Verification Lab: Claims, Sources & AI Reliability

Check AI-generated claims against evidence, sources, context, and uncertainty.

Week 5 — Algorithmic Fairness & Access

Analyze how seemingly neutral inputs can create unequal outcomes and propose safeguards.

Week 6 — Synthetic Media, Influence & Digital Citizenship

Evaluate potentially altered media before believing or sharing it and document verification decisions.

Week 7 — Human-Centered Problem Framing & AI Fit

Investigate an authentic need before deciding whether AI belongs in the solution.

Week 8 — Responsible AI Design Challenge

Refine a solution concept with minimum necessary data, human review, safeguards, limits, and accountability.

Sample lessons

See a lesson from this pathway

Spotting the Difference: AI or Human-Made?

Grade Band: Middle School Estimated Time: 45 minutes
Learning Objectives
Students distinguish common characteristics of AI-generated text and images from human-made work, and explain their reasoning.
Key Vocabulary
Artificial intelligence, generated content, bias, verification
Activity Overview
In small groups, students review paired examples of AI-generated and human-made content, discuss identifying clues, and present their reasoning to the class.
Responsible AI Connection
Reinforces the habit of evaluating AI-generated information rather than accepting it at face value.
Assessment Method
Group discussion rubric plus a short individual reflection.
Required Materials
Printed or projected content pairs (provided), discussion guide (provided).

Career-Connected

Plan a Responsible Drone Mission

Students define a mission goal, review safety and privacy considerations, plan a flight path, and explain how data may support an industry need.

Standards-informed: ISTE Standards for Students, CSTA K–12 CS Standards, AI4K12 Five Big Ideas, and digital citizenship & media literacy, with responsible-AI guidance from UNESCO and the NIST AI RMF.
