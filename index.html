One Connected K–12 Journey

The curriculum deepens as students grow

Three grade-band pathways share one responsible-AI foundation while increasing in independence, evidence, systems thinking, and career connection.

Current student workbooks are designed for Grades 3–5, 6–8, and 9–12. K–2 implementation is supported through teacher-led elementary adaptation guidance rather than a separate K–2 student workbook.

Curriculum Pillars

Six pillars, one connected learning path

AI Foundations

Core concepts of how AI systems work, in plain language appropriate for every grade band.

Creativity and Innovation

Using AI thoughtfully to support writing, design, inquiry, creativity, and problem-solving.

Responsible and Ethical AI

Examining fairness, bias, privacy, and the impact of AI on people and communities.

Prompting and Communication

Clear communication skills for working effectively and thoughtfully with AI tools.

Critical Thinking and Media Literacy

Evaluating AI-generated information and recognizing its strengths and limits.

Digital Citizenship and Future Readiness

Preparing students to navigate technology safely, ethically, and confidently.

Grade-Band Pathways

Three pathways. One developmental learning system.

Elementary School

AI Discoverers | Elementary School

Grades 3–5 • Six-week foundational pathway

Notice patterns, protect people, check information, create thoughtfully, and build foundational digital citizenship.

Middle School

AI Explorers | Middle School

Grades 6–8 • Eight-week Responsible AI & Digital Systems Lab

Analyze systems, audit data, verify evidence, evaluate impact, and design responsibly through a portfolio-based learning experience.

High School

AI Innovators | High School

Grades 9–12 • Nine-week AI literacy & readiness pathway

Research, verify, evaluate fairness, examine synthetic media, solve authentic problems, and build evidence through a responsible AI design portfolio.

Evidence of Learning

What students actually do

Future Ready AI is designed around observable student thinking and decision-making—not passive exposure to AI tools.

See the full curriculum architecture →

Elementary

Pattern Detectives

Students classify examples, compare rules, identify possible mistakes or unfair groupings, and explain why human review matters.

Middle School

Verification & evidence trail

Students compare claims and sources, document verification decisions, protect private information, and disclose meaningful AI assistance.

High School

Responsible decision review

Students analyze AI-supported decisions for missing context, fairness, accountability, and human oversight before recommending safeguards.

Career-Connected

Drone Career Explorer

Students connect mission planning, safety, aerial-data interpretation, teamwork, and career exploration in a Future Ready AI–aligned pathway.
